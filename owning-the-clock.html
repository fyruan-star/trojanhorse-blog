Owning theClock

UNDISCLOSED IS NOT ABSENCE
OF INFORMATION, IT IS SOME

Francis Ruan

When terms are not disclosed, the reflex is to assume the deal was small and move on. I have stopped doing that. Undisclosed is not an absence of information, it is a decision somebody made, and in this case I think the decision is more revealing than the number would have been. On the twenty ninth of April 2026, Infinite Epigenetics acquired Tally Health in an asset purchase, and I am the customer this one is about.

Read what actually changed hands

I get bloodwork drawn specifically to check whether the things I take are doing what they claim, which makes me precisely the person biological age testing is built for. So I read this announcement twice.

Tally Health was co founded in 2023 by the Harvard geneticist David Sinclair and sells an epigenetic biological age test alongside a supplement line. The buyer also owns TruDiagnostic and states that it holds among the largest private datasets of adult DNA methylation. Tally continues as a standalone brand under Melanie Goldey. The obvious description is that a longevity company bought a longevity company, and that description is filed under the wrong heading. My read is that what was acquired is a methylation dataset and a competing clock, and the supplement line came along attached to it.

A clock is not a measurement, and that distinction is everything

Biological age is not a measurement the way height is a measurement. It is a model. Somebody decides what counts as a year, which markers matter, and what the reference population looks like. Two clocks can read the same blood and disagree, and neither is lying.

This is where I would push back on how the field talks about itself. A biological age result is presented to consumers with the confidence of a bathroom scale, and it is nothing of the kind. If you already hold the largest reference dataset and you then buy the most consumer visible rival clock, you have not added a product to a catalogue. You have consolidated the benchmark, and every longevity intervention that wants to claim it works eventually validates against somebody's clock. There is now one fewer somebody.

My concern, and it is the personal one

Here is what actually bothers me as a customer rather than as an analyst. The company selling you the intervention now also owns the instrument grading whether the intervention worked.

I want to be careful and precise. I have no evidence of manipulation, nothing in the announcement suggests any intent of the kind, and vertical structures like this are common in diagnostics and not improper in themselves. But it is a conflict existing by design rather than by accident, and if I am paying to find out whether my own stack does anything at all, I would prefer the scorekeeper and the vendor were different people. That preference is not a scandal. It is just a preference I am allowed to have with my own money.

My call

The most strategically interesting deal on my list and the one carrying the least public information, which I do not think is a coincidence. What I will watch is narrow and checkable. Does Tally's test keep reporting on its own clock, or does it quietly migrate onto TruDiagnostic's? That single answer separates a portfolio addition from a benchmark consolidation, and I know which way I am leaning.

Whoever owns the clock decides what a year is worth, and there is now one fewer party deciding.

Sources

  1. Infinite Epigenetics announcement of the Tally Health asset purchase, 29 April 2026. Terms not disclosed by either party.
  2. Tally Health founding, leadership and product scope, and Infinite Epigenetics' ownership of TruDiagnostic, per the announcement.
