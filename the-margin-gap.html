TheMargin Gap

FIFTY NINE PERCENT AT THE TOP
EIGHT PERCENT BY THE BOTTOM

Francis Ruan

I train for a half Ironman and I race HYROX, so complete nutrition is a category I pay attention to as a person and not only as somebody reading filings. Danone announced its acquisition of Huel in March 2026 at around eight hundred and sixty million pounds and the CMA cleared it that August. The number that stopped me was not the price. It was the gap between two lines on Huel's own income statement.

Run the two lines

In the financial year to 2024 Huel recorded two hundred and fourteen million pounds of revenue at a fifty nine percent gross margin, and eighteen point two million pounds of adjusted EBITDA. Revenue reached two hundred and fifty million the following year, up sixteen percent.

Fifty nine percent of two hundred and fourteen million is about a hundred and twenty six million of gross profit. EBITDA came in at eighteen. So roughly a hundred and eight million pounds, close to half of all revenue, goes out somewhere below the gross line. In a direct to consumer subscription business, the overwhelming majority of that is the cost of finding the next customer.

What that tells me the business actually is

Huel is not expensive to make. Huel is expensive to sell. A fifty nine percent gross margin says the product economics are genuinely good, and an eight percent EBITDA margin says almost all of that good is being spent on acquisition.

My read is that Danone did not buy a food company. It bought a marketing bill it believes it can stop paying, because the thing Danone owns in abundance is the one thing Huel has been renting at enormous cost, which is access to the customer.

Why I think this synergy is real

I am usually sceptical of synergy arguments because they are the easiest slide in the deck to write and the hardest line to find afterwards. This one is different, because the mechanism is concrete rather than cultural. Shelf space substitutes directly for paid acquisition. You are not hoping two teams collaborate. You are swapping one cost line for an asset you already own.

That gives a test that needs no model. Watch whether EBITDA margin climbs from eight toward the gross margin over the next three years. If it does, the thesis was right. If it has not moved, Danone bought revenue at a food multiple and inherited the advertising budget along with it.

My concern

Retail is not free, it is just differently expensive, and a brand built on a subscription relationship does not always survive the move to a shelf where it sits beside eleven alternatives. The loyalty that made the DTC numbers work may be partly a function of the DTC format itself.

That is a hypothesis rather than a finding. But it is why I would watch subscriber retention as closely as I watch the margin.

My call

The best articulated thesis on my list, and the easiest to grade. Fifty nine into eight is the whole deal, and either Danone closes that gap or it did not need to own the company to find out.

Fifty nine percent at the gross line arriving as eight at EBITDA is not a food business, it is a customer acquisition business.

  1. Danone announcement March 2026; CMA clearance August 2026; consideration of approximately 860m from reporting.
  2. Huel FY2024 revenue, gross margin and adjusted EBITDA, and FY2025 revenue, per company figures reported in trade coverage.
