Trojan Horses in Tech Banking

Banks are welcoming private credit partnerships because they expand financing options for clients. They are adopting AI because it helps employees complete work faster. Both decisions offer immediate benefits. I think they also carry risks to two things an investment bank needs to survive: access to clients and the ability to develop experienced advisers.

In the Greek story, the Trojans brought a wooden horse inside their walls because they believed it was an offering left by the departing Greeks. The soldiers concealed inside emerged at night and opened the gates. Troy’s defenses failed because the Trojans admitted something they believed was safe.

The banking comparison begins with the reasons these developments are being welcomed. Private credit brings capital. AI brings efficiency. The question is whether accepting those benefits also creates a way to weaken the business from within.

What the two horses could change

Private credit partnerships and AI adoption can improve a bank’s results while changing how it competes in the future. One can give another institution a stronger relationship with its clients. The other can reduce the experience available to its future advisers.

That is my thesis: banks risk weakening their advisory businesses if they judge these developments mainly by the capital they provide and the costs they remove.

In the Greek story, the horse crossed the walls because the Trojans accepted the explanation for bringing it inside. Banks have substantial reasons to welcome both private credit and AI. They also need to examine what those decisions allow to happen afterward.
