Bought thenFreed

EVERYONE FILED THIS UNDER
NVIDIA BUYING THE STACK

Francis Ruan

The tidy version of this deal is that NVIDIA is buying up the AI stack layer by layer, and Run:ai is one more brick in the wall. I understand why that framing is attractive. It is also the opposite of what happened, because within a day of owning the thing NVIDIA said it would release it as open source, which is a genuinely strange way to hoard something.

The sentence that would not parse

NVIDIA agreed to buy Run:ai in April 2024 and closed that December after review by the Department of Justice and the European Commission. Reported price is around seven hundred million dollars, which neither party confirmed. Then they gave it away.

I reread that several times. Paying seven hundred million for something and immediately releasing it free is not a sentence that survives first contact with normal deal logic, and when a deal refuses to make sense on the obvious reading it usually means I am reading the wrong asset.

What Run:ai actually is, which explains everything

Run:ai orchestrates GPU workloads across Kubernetes clusters. It decides which job gets which slice of which chip, and it lets a single physical GPU be shared rather than handed whole to one task. GPUs are the most expensive objects in the building and they spend an embarrassing amount of time idle, so orchestration is what turns a pile of hardware into a utility.

That means orchestration sits one layer above the thing NVIDIA sells. It is a complement, not a product. And the correct move with a complement has been the same since Rockefeller was selling kerosene and giving away lamps: make it free, abundant and excellent, because every gram of friction you remove from the layer above lands as demand in the layer you own.

So the accumulation story is backwards

Free orchestration that runs anywhere still has to schedule onto something. My read is that NVIDIA spent seven hundred million to make certain the scheduling layer never became a place a competitor could get a grip, and then removed any commercial reason for anyone to build an alternative.

Against that balance sheet the price is close to a rounding error, and I think it is the cheapest defensive purchase on my list by a wide margin. Calling it accumulation misses that the strategic content is in the giving away, not the buying.

The part that cuts against NVIDIA

Open source is not a one way door. Software that orchestrates GPUs generically is also software that makes it easier to orchestrate somebody else's GPUs.

My concern is modest but real. The openness protecting the moat today lowers the switching cost tomorrow, and the parties most motivated to extend an open orchestrator are precisely the ones selling alternative silicon. I would not call that an error. I would call it a price NVIDIA looked at and decided it could afford.

My call

A good trade and a clearer statement of strategy than anything in the announcement. What I would watch is not adoption, because adoption is guaranteed once a useful thing becomes free. It is who is contributing in three years, and what hardware those contributors happen to sell.

The strategy was not in the buying. It was in giving it away the moment they owned it.

Sources

  1. NVIDIA announcement of the agreement, April 2024; completion December 2024 following US and EU review.
  2. The approximately seven hundred million figure is from reporting and was not confirmed by either party; some accounts place it nearer eight hundred million.
