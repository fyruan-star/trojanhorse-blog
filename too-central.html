Nvidia andArm

AN ASSET WHOSE VALUE DEPENDED
ON NOT BEING OWNED BY A COMPETITOR

Francis Ruan

Nvidia agreed in September 2020 to acquire Arm from SoftBank for up to $40 billion in cash and stock. In February 2022 the parties abandoned the transaction. SoftBank retained $1.25 billion that Nvidia had paid in advance.

The case for it

Nvidia was becoming the most commercially important designer of processors for data centre computing. Arm owned the instruction set most processors outside the personal computer market are built around. A combination would have given one company a position across a very wide range of computing.

SoftBank wanted to sell, the price was high, and both boards agreed terms.

Why it could not complete

Arm licenses its designs to companies that compete intensely with one another. Part of why they were willing to build on a common foundation is that Arm builds no chips and therefore has no stake in which of them wins.

Nvidia does build chips and competes with a significant number of Arm licensees. Several objected, publicly and in submissions to regulators in the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union. Qualcomm was among the more vocal. The objection was not that the combined company would hold too large a share of a defined market. It was that an interested owner would have visibility into competitors' plans and influence over their costs.

SoftBank listed Arm on Nasdaq in September 2023 instead, at a valuation well above the $40 billion Nvidia had offered.

The general point, with a complication

Where part of an asset's value rests on its independence, selling it to an interested party reduces the thing being bought. Standards bodies, marketplaces, benchmarks and ratings all share some of this property, and it does not appear in a financial model because there is no line for it.

The complication is worth stating. In March 2026 Arm announced a server processor of its own, which puts it in some tension with licensees building their own Arm-based chips. The independence that made the Nvidia sale impossible is a position Arm is now testing itself, and how its customers respond will say more about the strength of that argument than the 2022 outcome did.

Some assets are worth less once an interested party owns them, and the reduction does not appear anywhere in the valuation.
