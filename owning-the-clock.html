Owning theClock

NO PRICE WAS DISCLOSED
AND THE ASSET IS A DATASET

Francis Ruan

This is the one on my list that I keep thinking about after I close the tab, and it is because I am the customer. I get bloodwork drawn specifically to check whether the things I take are doing what they claim, which means I am exactly the person biological age testing is built for. On the twenty ninth of April 2026, Infinite Epigenetics acquired Tally Health in an asset purchase. Terms were not disclosed by either party, and I am not going to invent a multiple for a price nobody published.

Read what actually changed hands

Tally Health was co founded in 2023 by the Harvard geneticist David Sinclair and sells an epigenetic biological age test alongside a line of supplements. The buyer also owns TruDiagnostic and states that it holds among the largest private datasets of adult DNA methylation. Tally continues as a standalone consumer brand under Melanie Goldey.

So the obvious description is that a longevity company bought a longevity company. My read is that what was really acquired is a methylation dataset and a competing clock, and the supplement line came along attached.

Why a clock is not a measurement

Biological age is not a measurement in the way height is a measurement. It is a model. Somebody has to decide what counts as a year, which markers matter, and what the reference population looks like. Two clocks can look at the same blood and disagree, and neither of them is lying.

If you already hold the largest reference dataset and you then buy the most consumer visible rival clock, you have not added a product to a catalogue. You have consolidated the benchmark. Every longevity intervention that wants to claim it works eventually has to validate against somebody's clock, and now there is one fewer somebody.

My concern, and it is the personal one

Here is what actually bothers me as a customer rather than as an analyst. The company that sells you the intervention now also owns the instrument that grades whether the intervention worked.

I want to be careful and precise about this. I have no evidence of any manipulation, and nothing in the announcement suggests any intent of that kind. The structure is common in diagnostics and it is not inherently improper. But it is a conflict that exists by design rather than by accident, and if I am paying to find out whether my own stack does anything, I would like the scorekeeper and the vendor to be different people.

My call

The most strategically interesting deal on my list and the one with the least public information, which is not a coincidence. The thing I will watch is narrow and checkable. Does Tally's test keep reporting on its own clock, or does it quietly migrate onto TruDiagnostic's? That single answer tells you whether this was a portfolio addition or a benchmark consolidation, and I know which way I am leaning.

Whoever owns the clock decides what a year is worth, and right now that is one fewer party than it was.
