WhatTraining Requires

HOW A SOFTWARE ADVANCE BECAME
A QUESTION ABOUT ELECTRICAL CAPACITY

Francis Ruan

Something clearly changed in late 2022. The part that mattered commercially to the companies in this timeline was less the software itself than the computing it required, and the consequences of that requirement ran a long way into the physical world.

Training and inference, which are different jobs

Training is building the model. A system is shown a very large quantity of text or images and adjusts billions of internal values until it predicts well. This runs on thousands of processors for weeks or months and is expensive.

Inference is using the result. A question goes in and an answer comes out. Each instance is cheap and the total is not, because there are a great many questions.

Training resembles a construction project. Inference resembles a utility bill. Most of the spending described in the timeline between 2023 and 2025 is the first kind.

Why thousands of chips rather than one fast one

A conventional processor is built to perform operations one after another very quickly. A graphics processor is built to perform thousands of simple operations at the same time. It was developed for video games, where the colour of two million pixels has to be worked out simultaneously and the order does not matter.

Training a model has a similar shape. That is how a company whose products were originally sold for video games came to supply much of the hardware used for AI training.

One processor is not sufficient. A large training run uses thousands working together, which means the connections between them affect how much of that hardware is actually in use. It is why the Mellanox purchase appears in the timeline.

Where the constraint ends up

Thousands of processors in one building draw a great deal of electricity and convert nearly all of it into heat, so the building needs power delivered and heat removed. Both are matters of civil and electrical engineering rather than software.

By 2026 the constraint most often described by operators was electrical capacity and the time needed to obtain a grid connection. In several markets hardware can be purchased considerably faster than power can be connected.

Training large models turned a software advance into a demand for buildings, electrical capacity and cooling, on timescales set by utilities rather than by engineers.

Words you now own

  • ModelThe result of training. Billions of values encoding patterns in data.
  • TrainingBuilding the model. Thousands of processors, running for weeks.
  • InferenceUsing the model. Cheap individually, expensive in aggregate.
  • GPUA processor that performs many simple operations at once.
  • ParametersThe internal values a model adjusts during training.
