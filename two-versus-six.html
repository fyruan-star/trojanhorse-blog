Two versusSix

THE WELLNESS TRADE IS NOT
ONE TRADE, AND HERE IS PROOF

Francis Ruan

People talk about the wellness trade as though it were a single thing you could be long or short. I have never quite believed that, and this year handed me the cleanest disproof I am likely to get. One sponsor, one calendar year, two health brands riding the same demand, priced at roughly two times revenue going in and roughly six times coming out.

The two numbers

In January 2026 L Catterton took a majority stake in Good Culture, the cottage cheese brand, at a reported value above five hundred million dollars. Good Culture did about a hundred million of revenue in 2023, close to double that in 2024, and was tracking near two hundred and fifty million in 2025. That is about two times sales. Semcap Food and Nutrition added a further fifty five million that February.

In August the same firm sold Thorne to Procter and Gamble at a reported three point eight billion, against roughly six hundred and fifty million of forward revenue. About five point eight times.

Two explanations I had to discard

My first instinct was growth, and it was wrong. Good Culture roughly doubled. That is not a business being marked down for stalling.

My second was category, and that failed too. Both are things people put in their body deliberately, sold on a health claim, riding the same protein and longevity demand. If anything cottage cheese has had the louder cultural moment. So whatever is producing a four turn spread is not the thing the category label describes, which is exactly why I distrust the category label.

What I think it actually is

My read is that the spread is copyability and very little else. Cottage cheese is a refrigerated commodity with a cold chain, a short shelf life, and a private label version on the same shelf at a lower price. A competitor can match the product. The brand is real and the execution is plainly excellent, but the moat is thin by construction and no amount of growth thickens it.

Thorne's moat was a practitioner willing to say the name out loud to a patient who was frightened. You cannot private label that, and you cannot rebuild it once it has been spent. Four turns of revenue is what the market charges for that difference, and I find that a far more useful number than anything in the category commentary.

Where I would push back on myself

I am aware I may be reading a sponsor's portfolio timing as a market judgement. L Catterton bought one asset and exited another, and exit timing has as much to do with fund cycles and a closed IPO window as with anything I am claiming about moats.

I would rather name that objection than have it named for me. It is the strongest argument against my own read and I do not think it fully defeats it, because a fund cycle explains when you sell and not what somebody is willing to pay when you do.

My call

Two was right for Good Culture and six was right for Thorne, and the distance between them is the most useful single fact I have learned about this aisle all year. If you want to know what a consumer health brand is worth, do not ask how fast it is growing. Ask whether somebody could make it for less, and whether anyone would notice.

Ask whether somebody could make it for less, and whether anyone would notice. That is the multiple.
