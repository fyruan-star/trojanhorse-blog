Cisco andSplunk

TWO DIFFERENT BUYERS FOR THE SAME DATA,
AND A HARDWARE COMPANY SELLING SOFTWARE

Francis Ruan

Cisco agreed to acquire Splunk in September 2023 at $157 a share in cash, around $28 billion. It was Cisco's largest acquisition, announced in the weakest year for technology transactions in more than a decade, and completed in March 2024.

The product

Software records what it does. A user signed in. A request failed. A server restarted at four in the morning. These records are called logs, and a large organisation produces them in volumes no person could read.

Splunk collected logs from across an organisation's systems and made them searchable quickly. That is the product.

Who pays for it

Two groups, for different reasons, which is part of why the business held up.

Security teams pay because the evidence of an intrusion is distributed across many systems and the useful question is what happened in sequence. The category is known as security information and event management.

Engineering teams pay because applications are now assembled from many separate services, often maintained by different teams, so when something fails no individual holds the whole picture. Reading the record is the practical way to find out. That category is called observability.

A product serving two budgets within the same customer is harder to remove than one serving a single budget, because a cost reduction in one department does not by itself end the relationship.

The question this raises

Cisco paid cash at a time when cash was comparatively expensive, which indicates conviction, and bought when competition for assets was unusually low, which is a reasonable thing for a buyer to do.

The open issue is execution rather than price. Cisco has sold network hardware to these customers for decades on a purchasing cycle measured in years. Selling a software subscription is a different commercial motion with different staff, different renewal dynamics and different measures. Hardware companies have attempted this transition before with varied results, and the outcome here is not yet clear.

A product paid for out of two separate budgets inside the same customer is harder to displace than its category suggests.
