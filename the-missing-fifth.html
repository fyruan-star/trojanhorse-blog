TheMissing Fifth

EVERYONE QUOTED THE MULTIPLE
NOBODY CHECKED THE STAKE

Francis Ruan

I am not a neutral reader of this one. I take around fifteen things every day, I read the mechanism on each of them before they go in, and I get bloodwork drawn to check whether any of it is doing what it claims. So when Unilever completed its acquisition of Grüns on the first of June, my first thought was not about the multiple. It was that another shelf in my own category just changed owner.

The number everyone repeated

Grüns was founded in 2023 by Chad Janis, sells a daily greens supplement in gummy form almost entirely by subscription, reached roughly three hundred million dollars of annual revenue in under four years, and was reported profitable within about fourteen months. The deal came out at approximately one point two billion dollars, or seven hundred and sixty seven million euro.

Almost every write up then did the same arithmetic. One point two billion over three hundred million, so four times sales, so a sensible bolt on price. I did it too, and then I went looking for the share count, which is where it gets interesting.

Four fifths is not the whole thing

Trade reporting places that consideration against eighty percent of the shares rather than all of them. If that is right, the implied whole company value is closer to one and a half billion and the multiple is nearer five times, not four.

A full turn of revenue is not a detail. It is the difference between a bolt on price and a growth price, and those two numbers imply completely different expectations about what happens next. I want to be careful here, because I could not confirm the split in Unilever's own half year filing, which records the completion and says nothing about terms. So I am flagging it rather than banking it.

What I think the unpurchased fifth is for

My read is that leaving twenty percent outstanding is not thrift. It is a retention device. Grüns is three years old and founder run, and the entire asset is a subscription machine that somebody has been operating with unusual discipline. You do not buy that outright if what you actually need is for the operator to stay.

The deal term is doing more work than the headline, which is generally true and almost never written down.

My concern as somebody who buys this category

Here is what I actually care about. A greens gummy is a formulation, and formulations get revisited. Under a founder, the reason to change the formula is usually efficacy. Under a large consumer owner with a cost line to manage, the reason to change it can be margin.

I have no evidence that anything like that has happened or is planned, and I want to be clear that this is a concern rather than a finding. But it is the thing I will notice first, because I read labels for a living and for fun, and a quiet reformulation is exactly the kind of change that never makes an announcement.

My call

A good price for Unilever if the subscription base holds, and the subscription base holds as long as the product keeps doing what the people taking it think it does. That is a narrower dependency than four times revenue suggests.

The multiple everyone quoted was priced against four fifths of a company.

Sources

  1. Unilever Form 6-K, half year results to 30 June 2026, confirming completion in June 2026 and not stating terms.
  2. Consideration, revenue scale and the eighty percent stake are from trade reporting rather than the filing, and are treated here as reported rather than confirmed.
