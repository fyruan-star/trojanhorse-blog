Nvidia andMellanox

WHY THE CONNECTIONS BETWEEN MACHINES
DETERMINE THE VALUE OF THE MACHINES

Francis Ruan

Nvidia agreed to acquire Mellanox Technologies in March 2019 at $125 a share in cash, about $6.9 billion. It was Nvidia's largest acquisition to that point and completed in April 2020 after regulatory review.

What Mellanox sold

Mellanox made high speed networking hardware, principally for a technology called InfiniBand. This is not internet networking. It is designed for connecting machines inside a single facility, where delay is measured in microseconds and consistency matters more than distance.

Its customers were largely scientific computing centres. National laboratories, weather services, genomics institutes. The market was specialised and not large.

Why that equipment determines the value of other equipment

A computing job too large for one machine is divided across many. Those machines cannot work entirely independently, because each periodically needs results the others have produced. So they stop, exchange, and continue.

If the connection between them is slow or inconsistent, every machine waits for the slowest exchange. The practical effect is that an operator who has bought a facility full of expensive processors gets less work out of them. The industry measures this as utilisation, and at large scale the network often sets it.

This is why a networking supplier can charge well for what looks like commodity hardware. The customer is not comparing it against other cables. It is comparing the cost against idle time on equipment it has already bought.

Separating the purchase from what followed

Nvidia's stated rationale at announcement concerned data centre computing, already a growing part of its business. Intel was reported to have bid for the same asset.

Demand for training large AI models arrived several years later and made this capability far more valuable than it was in 2019. I would keep those two facts apart. The acquisition is adequately explained by what Nvidia's existing customers were already doing, and nothing in the announcement claims anticipation of what came after. Describing it as foresight makes it sound less repeatable than it was.

The supplier whose product determines how much use a customer gets from equipment it has already bought is in a stronger position than the category description suggests.
