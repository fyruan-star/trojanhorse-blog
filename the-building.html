TheBuilding Itself

THE HOTTEST ASSET IN TECHNOLOGY IS A SHED
WITH GOOD ACCESS TO A POWER LINE

Francis Ruan

A data centre is a warehouse full of computers, and describing it that way undersells how strange a business it has become. It is not really a technology asset. It is a property with an electricity contract, and in 2026 that is the scarcest thing in the industry.

What is actually in there

Rows of metal racks. Each rack holds servers, each server holds chips. Overhead, power. Underneath or alongside, cooling, because every watt that goes in comes out as heat and silicon stops working when it gets hot.

That is it. There is no magic floor. The interesting engineering is almost entirely about moving electricity in and moving heat out, which is why the people who build these increasingly come from industrial construction rather than from software.

The unit of account is the megawatt

Here is the tell that this is not a technology business any more. Data centres are not measured in square feet or in servers. They are measured in megawatts, which is a measure of electricity.

A megawatt is roughly what eight hundred homes draw. A large AI facility wants hundreds of them. You are not building a building. You are building something closer to a small town’s worth of demand, in one place, that runs flat out at three in the morning.

Water matters too, because a lot of cooling is evaporative, which has made data centres a local political issue in places that do not have much.

Why the money started going here

Follow the chain. A model needs thousands of chips. Thousands of chips need a building. The building needs power. Power needs a grid connection, and grid connections are issued by utilities on timescales measured in years.

So the bottleneck moved, step by step, from the clever part to the dull part, and the capital followed it. Spending that used to go to engineers now goes to concrete, transformers and industrial air conditioning. This is the single most important thing that happened to technology between 2023 and 2026, and it is almost never the headline.

Every AI company is now partly an energy company, whether it says so or not. The ones that admitted it early bought land near power.

Words you now own

  • Data centreA warehouse of computers. Measured in megawatts, not square feet.
  • RackThe metal frame the servers sit in. The basic unit inside.
  • MegawattA million watts. Roughly eight hundred homes. The real currency here.
  • Capex cycleA stretch of years where an industry spends heavily on physical things.
