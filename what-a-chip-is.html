WhatA Chip Is

Design, licensing and manufacturing are three separate businesses

Francis Ruan

A chip is designed, licensed and manufactured by different companies, and the manufacturing step is concentrated in so few places that governments treat it as a matter of national capability. That concentration is why regulators appear in several of the semiconductor entries in Ten Years, and it is the background a reader needs to see what each of those buyers was acquiring.

Three businesses

A semiconductor is a piece of silicon with a very small circuit printed on it. Billions of switches, each on or off, are arranged so that setting them in the right order performs arithmetic. Three kinds of company are involved in making one.

The first owns the architecture, which is the instruction set, the basic vocabulary a processor understands. Arm licenses this and manufactures nothing, and it receives a royalty on each chip built with its design. The second does the design, which is the specific layout of the chip. Nvidia, Apple and Qualcomm do this work and own no factories. The industry calls such a company fabless, and it is a business model and not a criticism. The third does the manufacturing, in a fabrication plant called a fab. A plant able to make the smallest features costs upward of twenty billion dollars and takes years to build.

Why manufacturing concentrates

Each generation of equipment costs more than the last, until only a handful of firms can afford to remain at the leading edge. The machines that print the smallest circuits use extreme ultraviolet light, and one company, ASML in the Netherlands, makes them. Each machine costs on the order of hundreds of millions of dollars, and the figure depends on the generation (CNBC).

The largest manufacturer by a considerable margin is TSMC, in Taiwan. Estimates of its share of the most advanced logic chips cluster around 90 percent, although sources define “most advanced” differently and the figure moves with the definition (EE Times Asia). A large share of the world's most advanced chips is therefore made in one place, and that place is the subject of an unresolved territorial dispute.

Why governments became parties to deals

The policy question is not whether chips are useful. It is whether a country can obtain them if relations deteriorate. That question explains why a committee reviewing foreign investment was able to stop a takeover in 2018, why export controls now cover equipment most people could not identify, and why several governments have committed large sums to building manufacturing capacity at home. The United States, for example, passed the CHIPS and Science Act in 2022 to fund domestic fabs.

The qualification is that concentration at manufacturing does not apply equally to every chip. Many chips used in cars and appliances are made on older equipment by many producers. The national capability argument is strongest for the leading edge, and weaker for the rest.

What a reader should take from it

When a semiconductor deal is announced, identify which of the three businesses is being bought, then check where the manufacturing happens and who owns the equipment. A design company, an architecture licensor and a manufacturer face different regulators, different customers and different risks. The development that would change this picture is a second or third source of leading-edge manufacturing in a different country, which would reduce the concentration the rest of this reasoning depends on.

Words you now own

  • SemiconductorA chip. Silicon with a circuit printed on it.
  • ArchitectureThe instruction set a processor is built around. Arm licenses this.
  • FablessA company that designs chips and owns no factory.
  • FoundryA company that manufactures chips it did not design. TSMC is the largest.
  • FabA fabrication plant. A leading-edge one costs tens of billions.
  • NodeThe size of the features being printed. Smaller is faster and harder to produce.
