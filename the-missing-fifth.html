TheMissing Fifth

AN INDUSTRY DID THE ARITHMETIC
AND NOBODY CHECKED THE STAKE

Francis Ruan

I am not a neutral reader of this one and I would rather say so upfront. I take around fifteen things every day, I read the mechanism on each of them before they go in, and I get bloodwork drawn to check whether any of it does what it claims. So when Unilever completed its acquisition of Grüns on the first of June, my first thought was not the multiple. It was that another shelf in my own bathroom just changed owner.

The arithmetic everybody performed

Grüns was founded in 2023 by Chad Janis, sells a daily greens supplement in gummy form almost entirely by subscription, reached roughly three hundred million dollars of annual revenue in under four years, and was reported profitable inside about fourteen months. The deal came out at approximately one point two billion dollars, or seven hundred and sixty seven million euro.

Then everyone, and I do mean nearly everyone, ran the same sum. One point two over three hundred, four times sales, sensible bolt on price, move along. I ran it as well. It took me an embarrassing amount of time to ask the obvious follow up question, which is four times sales of what exactly.

Four fifths of a company is not a company

Trade reporting places that consideration against eighty percent of the shares rather than all of them. If that holds, the implied whole company value is nearer one and a half billion and the multiple is closer to five times than four.

A full turn of revenue is not a rounding difference. It is the distance between a bolt on price and a growth price, and those imply completely different expectations about what has to happen next. What bothers me is not that the number moved. It is that an entire commentariat divided one figure by another without once asking what was in the numerator, and a press release ended up doing the analysis for the industry.

I want to be careful, because I could not confirm the split in Unilever's own half year filing, which records the completion and says nothing at all about terms. So I am flagging it rather than banking it. But a reported figure that nobody has interrogated is not a fact, it is a rumour with a decimal point.

What I think the unbought fifth is for

My read is that leaving twenty percent outstanding is not thrift. It is a retention device. Grüns is three years old and founder run, and the whole asset is a subscription machine somebody has been operating with unusual discipline. You do not buy that outright when what you actually need is for the operator to stay in the building.

The deal term is doing more work than the headline, which is true of most deals and almost never written down.

My concern, as somebody who buys this category

Here is what I actually care about, and it has nothing to do with Unilever's return. A greens gummy is a formulation, and formulations get revisited. Under a founder the reason to change a formula is usually efficacy. Under a large consumer owner with a cost line to manage, the reason can be margin.

I have no evidence that anything of the kind has happened or is planned, and I want that stated plainly rather than buried. This is a concern, not a finding. But it is the first thing I will notice, because I read labels for fun and a quiet reformulation is exactly the sort of change that never gets an announcement.

My call

A good price for Unilever if the subscription base holds, and the subscription base holds only as long as the product keeps doing what the people taking it believe it does. That is a much narrower dependency than four times revenue implies, and it is narrower still at five.

A reported number nobody has interrogated is not a fact. It is a rumour with a decimal point.

Sources

  1. Unilever Form 6-K, half year results to 30 June 2026, confirming completion in June 2026 and not stating terms.
  2. Consideration, revenue scale and the eighty percent stake are from trade reporting rather than the filing, and are treated here as reported rather than confirmed.
