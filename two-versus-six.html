Two versusSix

ONE SPONSOR, ONE YEAR
TWO TIMES IN, SIX TIMES OUT

Francis Ruan

I noticed this one because I had already spent weeks inside the other half of it. In January 2026 L Catterton took a majority stake in Good Culture, the cottage cheese brand, at a reported value above five hundred million dollars. In August of the same year, the same firm sold Thorne to Procter and Gamble at a reported three point eight billion. One sponsor, one calendar year, buying at roughly two times revenue and selling at roughly six.

The two numbers

Good Culture did about a hundred million dollars of revenue in 2023, close to double that in 2024, and was tracking near two hundred and fifty million in 2025. Above five hundred million on that base is about two times sales. Semcap Food and Nutrition put a further fifty five million in that February.

Thorne did over five hundred million in 2025 against roughly six hundred and fifty million tracked for 2026, which puts three point eight billion at around five point eight times forward revenue.

What the spread is not

My first instinct was that the gap is growth, and that instinct was wrong. Good Culture roughly doubled. That is not a slow business and it is not being priced like one because it is stalling.

It is also not category. Both of these are things people put in their body on purpose, sold on a health claim, riding the same protein and wellness demand. If anything, cottage cheese has had the louder cultural moment of the two.

What I think it is

My read is that the spread is copyability, and almost nothing else. Cottage cheese is a refrigerated commodity. It has a cold chain, a short shelf life, and a private label version sitting on the same shelf at a lower price. A competitor can match the product. The brand is real and the execution is clearly excellent, but the moat is thin by construction.

Thorne's moat was a practitioner willing to say the name out loud to a patient who was frightened. You cannot private label that and you cannot rebuild it once it is spent. Four turns of revenue is what the market charges for the difference.

Why I care about this one

This is the clearest natural experiment I have found for something I already thought. Same buyer, same year, same broad thesis about health, and the only variable that really moves is whether somebody with credentials is prepared to attach their reputation to the product.

My concern, and it is a genuine one, is that I may be reading a sponsor's portfolio timing as a market judgement. L Catterton bought one asset and exited another, and the exit timing has as much to do with fund cycles and a closed IPO window as with anything I am claiming. I would rather name that than have it named for me.

My call

Two is the right number for Good Culture and six was the right number for Thorne, and the distance between them is the most useful single fact I have learned about this aisle all year. If you want to know what a consumer health brand is worth, do not ask how fast it is growing. Ask whether somebody could make it for less and whether anyone would notice.

The multiple is answering one question. Can somebody make this for less, and would anyone notice.
