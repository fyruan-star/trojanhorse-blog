WHY A SOFTWARE BREAKTHROUGH TURNED INTO
AN ARGUMENT ABOUT ELECTRICITY

Francis Ruan

Everybody understood that something happened in late 2022. Almost nobody was told the part that mattered commercially, which is that the breakthrough did not create demand for programmers. It created demand for power stations.

Training and inference, which are not the same job

Training is building the model. You show a system an enormous amount of text or images and it adjusts billions of internal numbers until it gets good at predicting what comes next. This takes weeks or months, runs on thousands of chips at once, and costs somewhere between a lot and an astonishing amount.

Inference is using it. You ask a question, the model answers. That is cheap per question and ruinous at scale, because there are a great many questions.

Training is a construction project. Inference is a utility bill. The industry spent 2023 and 2024 on the first and is now discovering the second.

Why thousands of chips, and not one fast one

A normal processor, the kind in your laptop, is built to do one thing after another very quickly. A GPU is built to do thousands of simple things at the same time. It was invented for video games, where you need to work out the colour of two million pixels simultaneously and nobody cares what order you do it in.

Training a model turns out to be the same shape of problem. Which is how a company that made graphics cards for teenagers became the most valuable company in the world, and it is not a joke, it is just what happened.

But one GPU is not enough. You need ten thousand of them behaving like a single machine, which means the wiring between them matters as much as the chips. This is the entire reason the Mellanox deal is on the timeline.

And then it stops being a technology problem

Ten thousand chips in a room draw an extraordinary amount of electricity and convert nearly all of it into heat. So you need power, and you need to remove the heat, and both of those are civil engineering rather than software.

The constraint on artificial intelligence in 2026 is not ideas and it is not chips. It is grid connections. There are places where you can buy the hardware tomorrow and wait four years for the power.

The most advanced industry on earth spent three years discovering that its hard limit is the same one that constrained a nineteenth century steel mill. Somewhere to put it, and enough power to run it.

Words you now own

  • ModelThe thing that was trained. Billions of numbers that encode patterns.
  • TrainingBuilding it. Thousands of chips, weeks on end, enormous cost.
  • InferenceUsing it. Cheap each time, expensive by the billion.
  • GPUA chip that does thousands of simple things at once. Invented for games.
  • ParametersThe internal numbers a model adjusts. More is usually better and always costlier.
