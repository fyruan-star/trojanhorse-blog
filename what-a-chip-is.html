WhatA Chip Is

THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN DESIGNING ONE
AND MANUFACTURING ONE, AND WHY IT MATTERS

Francis Ruan

A semiconductor is a piece of silicon with a very small circuit printed on it. Billions of switches, each either on or off, arranged so that setting them in the right order performs arithmetic. Everything that follows is a consequence of how difficult that is to manufacture.

Three businesses, one chip

Designing a chip and making a chip are separate businesses, run by different companies in different countries. That separation explains a good deal of what appears in the timeline.

The architecture is the instruction set, the basic vocabulary a processor understands. Arm licenses this and manufactures nothing. It receives a royalty on each chip built using its design, which across the industry runs to tens of billions of units a year.

The design is the specific layout. Nvidia, Apple and Qualcomm do this work and own no factories. The industry calls such a company fabless, which sounds like a criticism and is a business model.

The manufacture happens in a fabrication plant, known as a fab. A plant capable of the smallest feature sizes costs upward of twenty billion dollars and takes years to build. Very few companies operate one at the leading edge, and the largest by a considerable margin is TSMC, in Taiwan.

Why governments became involved

The consequence is worth sitting with. A large share of the most advanced chips in the world are manufactured in one place, and that place is the subject of an unresolved territorial dispute.

This is the background to several entries in the timeline. It is why a committee reviewing foreign investment was able to stop a takeover in 2018, why export controls now cover equipment most people could not identify, and why several governments have committed large sums to building manufacturing capacity domestically. The policy question is not whether chips are useful. It is whether a country can obtain them if relations deteriorate.

A single supplier for one critical machine

The machines that print the smallest circuits use extreme ultraviolet light, and one company makes them. ASML, in the Netherlands. Each machine costs a few hundred million dollars and is delivered in several aircraft.

The result is a supply chain with very few alternatives at several steps. That concentration was not planned. It is what happens when each generation of equipment costs more than the last, until only a handful of firms can afford to remain at the leading edge.

Designing a chip, licensing the instruction set it is built on, and manufacturing it are three separate businesses with different economics. Several disputes in this timeline are about which of them was worth owning.

Words you now own

  • SemiconductorA chip. Silicon with a circuit printed on it.
  • ArchitectureThe instruction set a processor is built around. Arm licenses this.
  • FablessA company that designs chips and owns no factory.
  • FoundryA company that manufactures chips it did not design. TSMC is the largest.
  • FabA fabrication plant. A leading-edge one costs tens of billions.
  • NodeThe size of the features being printed. Smaller is faster and harder to produce.
