Premium withoutProfit

A PREMIUM IS A RATIO TO
A NUMBER SOMEBODY CHOSE

Francis Ruan

A hundred and seventy percent premium is the kind of number that ends an argument before it starts. Half the coverage treated it as evidence Mars had lost its mind and the other half as a triumph for shareholders, and I think both readings are lazy for the same reason. A premium is not a measure of value. It is a ratio to a starting point, and somebody chose that starting point.

What you cannot do here

Mars offered five hundred and thirty four million pounds for Hotel Chocolat in November 2023, three hundred and seventy five pence a share in cash, closing that January. In the financial year to July 2023 Hotel Chocolat had moved from a pre tax profit of twenty one point seven million pounds to a loss of eight hundred thousand.

So you cannot value this on earnings, because at the moment of the offer there effectively were not any. That is not a footnote, it is the whole puzzle. Whatever Mars was paying for was not a multiple of profit, and any take that starts from the premium is measuring from a price the market had already given up on.

My explanation, which is about structure rather than chocolate

Look at what does exist. Hotel Chocolat was founded in 2004, sells through its own stores and its own website, and owns a hundred and forty acre cocoa estate and the Rabot Hotel in Saint Lucia. That is a company controlling its product from the tree to the till.

My read is that Mars bought a structure it could not build. Mars is exceptional at manufacturing and at moving volume through other people's shelves. What it did not have is a premium brand with its own estate, its own retail, and a direct line to the person eating the thing. Vertical integration is slow, expensive and essentially impossible to assemble in pieces, so when you decide you want it you have to buy somebody who already did the twenty years. Framed that way the premium stopped looking irrational to me and started looking like the market had simply been pricing the wrong thing.

What makes me hesitate

The quality that makes Hotel Chocolat worth a premium is that it is small enough to control end to end. Every move Mars makes to scale it, more stores, more countries, more volume pushed through a finite estate, is a step toward dissolving the thing that justified the price.

My concern is not that Mars damages the brand on purpose. It is that the instinct making Mars excellent, push volume through distribution, is the precise instinct that unwinds a vertically integrated premium business. I have no evidence that is happening. It is the tension I would be watching, and I would watch it before I watched revenue.

My call

Defensible, and considerably more interesting than the premium makes it sound. The metric I would track is not sales growth, it is the share of sales still moving through Hotel Chocolat's own channels. If that falls while total sales climb, Mars bought a brand and quietly let go of the reason it was worth buying.

You cannot pay a multiple of earnings for a company that stopped earning, so the premium is telling you what else was in the box.

Sources

  1. Mars offer announced 16 November 2023 at 375p per share; transaction completed 25 January 2024.
  2. Hotel Chocolat financial year to July 2023, pre tax result moving from a 21.7m profit to a 0.8m loss, per contemporaneous coverage of the company's results.
