Paid inPaper

THE MOST USEFUL THING TO UNDERSTAND
ABOUT A NUMBER ON A PRESS RELEASE

Francis Ruan

AMD agreed to buy Xilinx in October 2020 in an all-stock transaction valued at about $35 billion. A few years earlier AMD had been close enough to failure that its survival was a live question. Understanding how it could possibly afford this is the most useful lesson on the whole timeline.

All-stock means nobody wrote a cheque

AMD did not pay $35 billion. It printed shares and handed them over. Xilinx shareholders stopped owning Xilinx and started owning a slice of the combined company.

AMD’s share price had risen roughly tenfold in the preceding few years. Its own paper had become extremely valuable, so it could buy a very large company without touching a bank, a loan, or a dollar it had earned.

This is not a trick. It is the ordinary mechanics of a bull market, and it is why acquisitions cluster at the top. The currency is strongest exactly when everything is most expensive.

So the headline number is a photograph

Here is the part that should change how you read every future announcement. In an all-stock deal, the value quoted on the day is a photograph of one day’s share price, multiplied by an exchange ratio.

The ratio is fixed. The share price is not. AMD and Xilinx announced at about $35 billion and closed in February 2022 at closer to $49 billion, because AMD stock had kept rising in between. Nothing about Xilinx changed. The price did, by fourteen billion dollars, for reasons entirely external to the asset being bought.

Anybody who drops a headline number like that into a comparison of what companies in a sector are worth is quoting a stale share price for the buyer and calling it a valuation of the target. It is the cheapest error in the business and it is in print most weeks.

Here is what was in the horse

The asset itself was an odd one. Xilinx made FPGAs, chips you can reconfigure after you have bought them. Slower than a purpose-built chip, far more flexible, useful when the thing you need to compute keeps changing.

In 2020 the obvious read was telecoms equipment, where Xilinx was strong. What was in the belly was that the AI buildout would want adaptable silicon in the network and at the edges, and would want a credible second source to NVIDIA badly enough to pay for it.

AMD became that second source. The acquisition it paid for in inflated paper turned out to be the base of a data centre business.

The number in the headline is not a price. It is one day's share price wearing a price's clothes, and in a bull market it is flattering everybody involved.

  2. Completed 14 February 2022. The higher closing value reflects AMD share price appreciation between announcement and completion and is widely reported at around $49bn.
