Ten Years

Katabasis

Greek, the going down. Ten years of technology coverage read from the surface to wherever we are now, on the understanding that every one of these deals was a horse somebody wheeled through their own gates.

2016

The finish line

Smartphone shipments stop growing. Every human who was going to buy one had bought one. You sprint at a finish line for a decade, cross it, and find there is just more field.

Acquisition · $32bn · July

SoftBank / ARM

Read everywhere as a very expensive bet on phones, by a man famous for very expensive bets.

In the bellyARM does not make chips. It licenses the blueprint and collects a royalty every time somebody stamps it into silicon. That blueprint was about to walk into the data centre, which was Intel’s house, and into nearly every AI accelerator built since.

2017

Acquisition · $15.3bn · March

Intel / Mobileye

Intel paying roughly forty times revenue for a company that makes cameras for cars, which made a lot of people cough.

In the bellyIntel had missed the phone completely and knew it. This was never really about cars. It was a very large cheque written in order to not miss the next one, and the next one turned out to be anything that computes while moving.

Carve-out · ~$18bn · September

Toshiba Memory

A struggling Japanese conglomerate selling a division to cover losses somewhere else.

In the bellyThe division made the memory chips that store everything. Who owns memory turns out to be a national question rather than a commercial one, which is why a straightforward sale took a year and involved three governments.

2018

Blocked · $117bn · March

Broadcom / Qualcomm

The largest technology takeover ever attempted. Hostile, fully financed, and on its way.

In the bellyIt was stopped by a presidential order, on the advice of a committee most people in technology could not have named. Not by a board. Not by a price. Chips stopped being a product that day and became territory, and the export controls, the subsidies and the fabs in Arizona were all already in that belly.

Abandoned · $44bn · July

Qualcomm / NXP

A finished deal waiting on one last signature.

In the bellyThe signature was Chinese and it never came. Qualcomm paid NXP $2bn to walk away from its own deal. The lesson a whole generation of dealmakers took from it: in semiconductors, the final approval is a foreign policy decision.

Acquisition · $34bn · October

IBM / Red Hat

An old company paying an enormous premium for a company that gives its software away free.

In the bellyRed Hat’s bet was that no serious business would put everything in one cloud. IBM was buying the layer that sits across all of them. It is the same move Google makes seven years later with Wiz, for almost exactly the same money.

2019

Acquisition · $6.9bn · March

NVIDIA / Mellanox

A networking bolt-on. Cables and switches, the equipment that lets one computer talk to another computer, which sits somewhere below the paper clip on any list of thrilling technologies.

In the bellyWhen you train an AI model you do not use one chip. You use ten thousand, and you need all ten thousand to behave like a single enormous brain. The thing that makes them behave like one is not the chips. It is the wiring between them. NVIDIA bought the wiring three years before anybody needed it, for less than it would later clear in a single quarter.

Acquisition · $15.7bn · June

Salesforce / Tableau

A software company buying a charts company.

In the bellyNobody switches software because of the software. They switch because of where their data already sits, and moving it is a year of somebody’s life. Salesforce was not buying charts. It was buying gravity.

2020

Free money

Central banks take rates to roughly zero and leave them there. When money costs nothing, anything with growth in it gets more expensive and patience gets cheaper. Every deal for the next two years has this one fact sitting underneath it.

Acquisition · $35bn · October

AMD / Xilinx

AMD, which had been close to bankrupt a few years earlier, buying a thirty-five billion dollar company.

In the bellyIt paid entirely in its own shares, which had risen roughly tenfold. This is the most useful thing to understand about stock-funded deals: the number on the press release is a photograph of one day’s share price, not a cheque anybody wrote.

2021

Peak

The busiest year in the history of dealmaking. Record volumes, a blank-cheque company for every idea somebody had in a bar, and an unshakeable sense that this was simply how things were now. It was the top. Nobody rings a bell at the top.

Acquisition · $19.7bn · April

Microsoft / Nuance

Microsoft buying the company behind the speech recognition your doctor mutters into.

In the bellyHospital data is the hardest data in the world to get anywhere near, for extremely good reasons. Microsoft bought a company that was already inside the room, about eighteen months before anybody worked out what a language model would be worth with access to that room.

2022

The window shuts

Rates rise faster than at any point in forty years. The Nasdaq falls about a third. Companies that were going to go public stop going public and stay private, which is where a great deal of this story now lives and where very little of it is visible.

Abandoned · $40bn · February

NVIDIA / ARM

The deal that would have handed one company the design nearly every other chip is built from.

In the bellyRegulators on three continents said no, and what actually killed it was that ARM’s customers were NVIDIA’s competitors. ARM listed separately instead and is now worth a great deal more than $40bn, which is the most expensive compliment ever paid to an antitrust lawyer.

Announced · $61bn · May

Broadcom / VMware

An enormous cheque written straight into a closing door. Completed in November 2023, after eighteen months of regulators.

In the bellyBroadcom’s model is not growth. It buys infrastructure software that large companies cannot realistically switch away from, raises the price, and removes everything that is not the price. It is one of the most reliable money machines in technology and almost nobody describes it out loud.

2023

A chatbot appears

ChatGPT crosses from a demo into every boardroom on earth. It looked like a toy you could ask to write a poem. What was actually in it was a capital expenditure supercycle, and within two years the most contested resource in technology would be electricity.

The trough

And yet the quietest year for technology dealmaking since the financial crisis. Bankers call this a dislocation. Everybody else calls it nothing happening.

Acquisition · $28bn · September

Cisco / Splunk

Cisco paying up for a company that reads log files, which is as unglamorous as it sounds.

In the bellyEvery machine writes down what it just did. Splunk reads all of it. In a year when almost nobody could tell you what their own systems were actually doing, the company that reads the record quietly became strategic.

2024

The money changes shape

Spending stops going to engineers and starts going to concrete, transformers and industrial air conditioning. The hottest asset in technology becomes a shed with good access to a power line.

Announced · ~$1.46bn · March

Keysight / Spirent

Test equipment. Genuinely, the machines that check whether other machines work. Completed October 2025.

In the bellyYou cannot build a network moving this much data without being able to prove that it moves. Picks and shovels, except this is the company that inspects the shovels, and in a buildout this large somebody has to.

2025

Acquisition · $32bn · March

Google / Wiz

The largest purchase of a private technology company ever made.

In the bellyWiz runs on Amazon’s cloud and Microsoft’s as well as Google’s, and Google committed publicly to keeping it that way. Google is a durable third in cloud. It did not buy a feature for its own estate. It bought the security layer sitting above everybody’s, including the two it keeps losing to.

Investment · $300m at $5bn · January

DDN / Blackstone

A private equity cheque into a storage company founded in 1998. Storage. The dullest available word in this entire industry.

In the bellyA GPU does not think slowly. It waits. The most expensive processor ever mass produced sits idle in the dark because the thing it needs to look at next has not arrived from storage yet. The bottleneck in artificial intelligence was never intelligence. It was fetching.

Where the money actually comes from

Convertible notes at Datadog. Exchangeable notes at Seagate. A term loan at LendingTree. None of this makes a headline and all of it is how the buildout gets paid for. A convertible note is a loan that is allowed to change its mind and become shares instead. Companies like them because the interest is cheap. Lenders like them because if the stock runs they stop being a lender and start being an owner.

2026

Acquisition · $11.9bn

IBM / Confluent

A data plumbing company, bought by IBM, for nearly twelve billion dollars.

In the bellyAn AI agent acting on your behalf cannot work from last night’s data. It needs what is true right now. Confluent moves data while it is still happening, and that turns out to be the whole difference between a model that answers you and a model that does something.

Rust is back

Hard drives were declared dead a decade ago. Flash was faster and had no moving parts and that was supposed to be the end of it. Then machines that read everything needed somewhere enormous and cheap to keep the things they might want later, and the spinning platter became strategic again. Seagate, left for dead, spent the year raising money on terms usually reserved for growth companies.

Here

Which is roughly where you are standing. Over ten years the industry stopped buying software and started buying physics, and the question underneath every single thing on this spine has been the same one. What is in the horse.

The spine is the money. It thickens as capital floods in and thins when the window shuts, drawn to the shape of the cycle rather than to a dataset, because an exact one would imply a precision this does not have. Deal values are as announced. Where a deal closed at a different time or a different number, the entry says so.
