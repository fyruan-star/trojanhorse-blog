InsideA Data Centre

A PROPERTY WITH AN ELECTRICITY CONTRACT,
MEASURED IN MEGAWATTS

Francis Ruan

A data centre is a building full of computers. Describing it that way understates how unusual the business has become, because the scarce input is not the computers. It is the power and the land to put them on.

What is in the building

Rows of metal racks. Each rack holds servers, each server holds processors. Above them, electrical distribution. Alongside, cooling, because nearly every watt delivered emerges as heat and processors fail when they get too hot.

Most of the engineering difficulty is in moving electricity in and heat out. That is why the people building these facilities increasingly come from industrial construction rather than from software.

Why these are measured in megawatts

Data centres are not usually described in square feet or in numbers of servers. They are described in megawatts, a measure of electrical power.

A megawatt is roughly the draw of eight hundred homes. A large facility built for AI training may want several hundred. That is comparable to the demand of a small town, concentrated in one place and running close to full load through the night.

Water is a consideration as well, because a good deal of cooling works by evaporation. That has made siting a local political matter in regions where water is limited.

Why spending moved this way

The sequence is straightforward. A model needs many processors. Many processors need a building. The building needs power. Power requires a grid connection, and grid connections are granted by utilities on timescales measured in years.

The constraint moved step by step toward the physical end of that chain, and spending followed it. A growing share of capital that would once have gone to software development went instead to construction, electrical equipment and cooling. It is among the more consequential developments of the period and it is reported considerably less than the models are.

A data centre is better understood as a property with an electricity contract than as a technology asset, and operators who secured power early held an advantage that was difficult to copy.

Words you now own

  • Data centreA building of computers. Measured in megawatts of power draw.
  • RackThe frame servers are mounted in.
  • MegawattA million watts, roughly the draw of eight hundred homes.
  • Capex cycleA period in which an industry spends heavily on physical assets.
